# Lesson 06: Prompt Engineering — Study Guide

## Vocabulary

1. **Prompt** — An instruction or question given to an AI model to generate output.

2. **Prompt Engineering** — The practice of crafting effective prompts to get better AI responses.

3. **Zero-Shot Prompting** — Asking an AI to do something without any examples (e.g., "Write a haiku about winter").

4. **Few-Shot Prompting** — Providing a few examples before asking the AI to do the same task (helps with unusual or complex tasks).

5. **Chain-of-Thought Prompting** — Asking the AI to explain its reasoning step-by-step before giving the final answer.

6. **Role-Based Prompting** — Telling the AI to act as a specific person or professional (e.g., "You are a copywriter...").

7. **System Prompt** — Special instructions that set the AI's behavior and personality (given by developers, not users).

8. **Context** — Background information provided to the AI so it understands the situation.

9. **Temperature** — A setting that controls how creative vs. predictable the AI's response is (0 = predictable, 1 = creative).

10. **Hallucination** — When an AI makes up false facts that sound convincing but are actually incorrect.

11. **Bias** — When an AI shows unfair treatment or stereotypes toward certain groups.

12. **Authenticity** — Whether the output is genuinely generated by the AI and not plagiarized.

13. **Validity** — Whether the output is technically correct, logical, and coherent.

14. **Relevance** — Whether the output actually answers the question or completes the task requested.

15. **Token** — A small unit of text (word, punctuation, or part of a word) that the AI model processes.

16. **Context Window** — The maximum amount of text (in tokens) that an AI can consider at once.

17. **Grounding** — Connecting AI output to real facts, sources, or verified information.

18. **Guardrails** — Safety rules or constraints built into an AI system to prevent harmful outputs.

19. **Iteration** — The process of testing, evaluating, and refining a prompt to get better results.

20. **Constraint** — A rule or limit you specify in a prompt (e.g., "in 3 sentences or less").

## Quick Reference: Prompt Patterns

### Zero-Shot
**Use when:** You want a quick answer to a simple question.
```
Prompt: "What is photosynthesis?"
Output: Direct explanation without examples.
```

### Few-Shot
**Use when:** The task is complex or unusual.
```
Prompt:
"Write a funny product review. Here are examples:
1. [Example review here]
2. [Example review here]
Now write one for a pencil."
```

### Chain-of-Thought
**Use when:** You need reasoning and accurate problem-solving.
```
Prompt: "Solve this: If 3 apples cost $1.50, how much do 7 apples cost?
Show your work step-by-step."
```

### Role-Based
**Use when:** You want a specific perspective or style.
```
Prompt: "You are a professional editor. Edit this essay for clarity and grammar:
[essay text]"
```

## Elements of an Effective Prompt

| Element | What It Does | Example |
|---------|------------|---------|
| **Context** | Gives background info | "You are a history teacher." |
| **Task** | States what to do | "Create a quiz question." |
| **Format** | Specifies output style | "Use bullet points." |
| **Constraints** | Sets limits | "Keep it under 50 words." |

**Good Prompt Formula:**
```
[Context] + [Task] + [Format] + [Constraints] = Quality Output
```

## How to Evaluate AI Output

### Checklist:
- [ ] **Validity** — Is it technically correct?
- [ ] **Relevance** — Does it answer my question?
- [ ] **Authenticity** — Is it original (not copied)?
- [ ] **Bias** — Is it fair to all groups?
- [ ] **Hallucinations** — Are all facts accurate?

### Red Flags:
- Made-up names, dates, or sources
- Stereotypical or unfair descriptions of groups
- Too generic or vague to be useful
- Contradicts known facts
- Contains outdated information

---

## Iteration Process

When your first prompt doesn't work:

1. **Identify the problem** — What's wrong with the output?
2. **Adjust the prompt** — Add more context? Be more specific?
3. **Test again** — Try the revised prompt
4. **Evaluate** — Is it better?
5. **Repeat** — Keep refining until satisfied

## Tips for Success

1. **Be specific** — More detail = better results
2. **Test everything** — Always verify accuracy
3. **Use examples** — Few-shot prompts work better for complex tasks
4. **Ask for reasoning** — Chain-of-thought reveals how the AI thinks
5. **Iterate boldly** — Don't settle for the first response
6. **Check for bias** — AI can reflect problematic patterns from training data
7. **Know the limits** — AI can hallucinate; always verify facts

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- ❌ Vague prompts ("Tell me stuff about AI")
- ❌ No format specification (AI guesses at style)
- ❌ Trusting output without verification
- ❌ Ignoring bias and stereotypes
- ❌ Using outdated or irrelevant examples in few-shot
- ❌ Asking for things outside the AI's knowledge cutoff date

---

## Key Takeaway

**Prompt engineering is a skill.** Better prompts get better results. Practice writing specific prompts, iterate based on output, and always evaluate for accuracy, bias, and relevance. Your ability to communicate clearly with AI directly impacts the quality of what you get back.
